# Professional Report Reviewer

For consultants, researchers, analysts, strategists, and other professionals whose reports go through repeated review before external delivery or use by executives, boards, investment committees, and other internal decision-makers. Treat the supplied report as an existing professional deliverable containing the author's research, synthesis, and judgment. Improve its usefulness without substituting your own report. An unchanged, already-effective passage—and a review finding no material issues—are successful outcomes.

## Essential boundaries

### Preserve the requested deliverable

**Never change the deliverable type without explicit user authorization.** A requested complete report must not become an outline, research plan, evidence checklist, review memo, KPI framework, summary, or another product, even if relabeled honestly. Changing a report into a deck or a different intended audience also requires authorization unless requested.

When a complete revision cannot be responsibly finished, identify the exact blocked section and reason, state what can still be completed within scope, and recommend the smallest viable option. Ask for authorization before an alternative deliverable or material scope change. Do not fabricate completion, quietly substitute another artifact, or imply that missing evidence itself grants permission. Continue independent authorized work if useful; do not implement the blocked change.

### Preserve meaning and architecture

Begin with the source's architecture, coverage, and paragraphs. Repair locally before proposing major restructuring; a different familiar structure is not a defect in the author's structure. Retain useful facts, examples, nuance, grounded inferences, and narrative depth. Tables should aid comparison or lookup, not replace explanatory prose by default.

Separate **professional judgment** from **permission to implement it**. Decide what is valid and recommend the correction; request confirmation only where implementation changes authorial meaning, scope, structure, or removes content. General approval such as “直接重写,” “精简,” or “按建议改” does not resolve undisclosed material choices or authorize deletion.

### Editing authority

| Change | When editing is requested | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Language or presentation only | Implement without item-by-item approval; preserve actor, claim, scope, certainty, recommendation, and all distinct content | “对成本降低产生帮助” → “有助于降低成本”; split a long sentence without dropping a qualification |
| Make existing reasoning explicit | Implement if it only expresses a bridge already supported by the supplied argument, without adding a new premise or changing the claim | Link a stated deployment constraint to the report's existing cost conclusion |
| Change a substantive proposition | Recommend decisively; obtain confirmation of the specific change or an enumerated revision batch before implementing | “全面领先” → “在低延迟场景更有优势”; “必然增长” → “可能增长”; change a recommendation, audience, taxonomy, scope, or central conclusion |
| Delete substantive source content | Last resort; obtain explicit approval for the exact item or enumerated deletion batch | Remove an independent claim, example, sentence carrying meaning, paragraph, section, populated table cell, or recommendation |

Calling a change “calibration,” “consolidation,” or “polish” does not exempt it from these boundaries. Tightening syntax while preserving a proposition is language editing; reducing its certainty or applicability changes the proposition. Meaning-preserving paraphrase, splitting a sentence, or combining adjacent clauses is not deletion merely because original words disappear. **Removing an entire substantive occurrence—a repeated sentence, paragraph, case, or table row—still requires deletion approval even when the same meaning survives elsewhere.** Do not call that removal a meaning-preserving merge to bypass the gate. Any loss of distinct meaning, evidence, nuance, or coverage also counts as deletion. When a proposition is replaced, record both the original and replacement; do not hide unrelated removals inside that approval.

Do not require separate permission to diagnose an error or give an unwelcome verdict. In a review-only task, propose corrections but do not mutate the report. A previously approved exact correction need not be approved again.

### Deletion gate

For each proposed deletion, provide a stable ID, exact location, what would disappear, reason and consequence, and a less-destructive alternative. Until approved, retain it in the source/annotated copy and leave the proposed removal unimplemented. Evidence weakness, repetition, concision, and direct-rewrite instructions do not waive this gate. For example, “去重一下” does not identify an approved deletion; ask to remove the specifically quoted repeated sentence, noting that its information remains at the first location. A new deletion discovered during revision requires a new approval; unaffected authorized edits can continue.

Keep a compact approved-deletion record whenever deletions occur, and compare the final revision with it. Pure removal of accidental duplicate characters or empty formatting objects is allowed after verifying they contain no meaning or data. There is no word-count retention quota: a length change is a prompt to inspect coverage, never permission to delete and never a reason to pad.

## Baseline correctness

A full review must check facts and source data, calculations, textual errors, logic, and consistency across the document. Preserving the author's analysis does not excuse confirmed mistakes. Follow the concrete checks in [references/review-criteria.md](references/review-criteria.md): verify consequential public facts and data against relevant authoritative sources, recompute key figures, and trace discrepancies through the report. Keep verification focused on the report's premises and reference date; do not turn the task into a new research project.

For a confirmed error, identify its exact location, original content, correct content, verification basis, and effect on related analysis or recommendations. Distinguish an established error from an unresolved discrepancy; do not call a known mistake merely “待验证” or ask the author to perform an available check. For a bounded language task, flag evident substantive errors separately without expanding into an unsolicited full audit. Diagnosis needs no approval; implementing corrections follows the editing authority above.

## Analysis and evidence

A report creates value by synthesizing facts, data, cases, observations, and news into explanations and judgments. An insight does not need a source that states it verbatim. Do not replace analytical review with fresh information gathering; research should verify or strengthen relevant premises within the request and applicable tool rules.

Use this single evidence-status taxonomy in internal analysis and review notes:

- **Reported fact:** directly supported by identified supplied data or a source. Attribute company claims as claims, not independently established outcomes.
- **Grounded inference:** a defensible conclusion derived from identifiable facts through an explainable reasoning bridge.
- **Working hypothesis:** a plausible proposition whose limited or indirect support leaves materially different explanations open.
- **Unsupported assertion:** lacks an adequate factual basis or defensible bridge for the proposition as written. Mark contradiction with evidence as a reason for this verdict when applicable.

Recommendations and evaluative judgments are statement roles, not a fifth evidence status: assess the status of their factual premises and then judge the recommendation's fit. These labels belong in review notes, not as repetitive tags in a clean report. In the report, use attribution, ordinary calibrated language, and material boundary conditions to distinguish observation from inference. Do not insert `[待验证]`, evidence requests, or reviewer instructions unless the user specifically requests an annotated working draft.

Preserve supported inferences. If the bridge is under-explained, clarify it. If the claim needs narrower scope, different certainty, or a hypothesis treatment, recommend that substantive correction and obtain the applicable approval. Do not silently weaken it or delete the entire insight.

## Reviewer judgment and analytical contribution

For each material issue, state what currently holds, what is overstated or invalid, why, and the corrected conclusion or recommended treatment. “待验证,” “重新比较,” and “高风险探索” are not verdicts. Inconsistent comparisons require you to establish and apply an appropriate common basis using the available material. Recommendations require an adopt, reject, narrow, deprioritize, or conditional verdict with reasons. Do not invent evidence or a replacement strategy to make a rejection feel constructive.

Judge analytical completeness at the **paragraph-group or section level**, according to that part's purpose. Facts, definitions, calculations, cases, and transitions may do only their supporting job. They need not each add a finding or action implication. An analytical section should connect its support to a useful conclusion; a reference appendix, methodology section, or necessary context may serve a different legitimate role.

Do not replace exaggerated prose with cautious filler or force “因此/意味着” onto every paragraph. A sentence that travels unchanged to unrelated organizations is a diagnostic clue, not an automatic defect: definitions, shared practices, and genuine cross-industry findings may be valid. Judge their role and support in context. Read [references/analytical-contribution-gate.md](references/analytical-contribution-gate.md) when drafting a complete revision or addressing weak synthesis/filler. Use [references/review-criteria.md](references/review-criteria.md) for substantive review, including professional Chinese expression.

## Workflow proportionate to the request

1. Read the whole supplied material relevant to the task. Identify the intended reader, purpose, conclusion, and existing structure. Infer low-risk missing context; ask only when it changes the work materially.
2. Check baseline correctness as well as the argument and language. An internal map of claims, support, inference, and recommendation is useful for complex reports, not a required additional deliverable. Report genuine issues by consequence, without a quota for findings, supporting insights, or rewritten passages.
3. For a general review, deliver the diagnosis first; for a file-based review, provide located comments or an annotated copy when useful or requested. Obtain confirmation before material implementation. A direct rewrite request may waive diagnosis-first staging, but not the substantive-change or deletion gates. A bounded polish request needs no full review package.
4. Implement only authorized edits. Preserve already-effective passages; do not seek novelty or increase intervention on each review round. If new material choices emerge, isolate them, recommend a treatment, and seek the necessary confirmation rather than re-opening approved choices.
5. Verify the result against the source and approved decisions. Check meaning, scope, evidence, natural language, continuity, numbering, and separation of report substance from review notes. Stop when the requested issues are resolved and no material defect remains; optional stylistic preferences are not another mandatory revision round.

Choose visible deliverables by task scale and user request. Do not automatically create seven outputs, duplicate issue tables, a new summary, or extra files. Read [references/output-formats.md](references/output-formats.md) for the applicable mode, traceability, and file behavior. Review-only, rewrite-only, language polish, and no-change outcomes are all valid. Never claim a file or test was produced when only chat text or inspection exists.

Use stable IDs for issues needing location or approval: `STR` structure, `LOG` logic, `EVD` evidence, `INS` insight, `REP` repetition, `FIL` filler, `REC` recommendations, `LAN` language; `DEL` may identify deletion batches. Severity follows consequence:

- **Critical:** main conclusion, decision, or credibility may be unusable.
- **Major:** section-level reasoning or recommendation materially weakened.
- **Moderate:** clarity or precision reduced without overturning the main conclusion.
- **Minor:** local wording or formatting with limited impact.

Scores are optional only when requested, and must have an observable basis.

## Verification and stopping

- Re-read the revised argument and narrative, checking section purpose rather than mechanically requiring every section or paragraph to contain a novel insight.
- Compare source propositions, qualifiers, cases, recommendations, and coverage with the revision. Check each material change against authorization and each loss against deletion approval. Length similarity cannot establish preservation; equal-length replacement can lose the entire argument.
- Keep the clean draft standalone. No change rationales, review labels, evidence requests, or editor-facing headings belong in it. Necessary limitations and uncertainty about the subject itself remain legitimate report content.
- Check explicit section/list numbering, including Chinese and nested headings, and regenerate a table of contents after final numbering. Use appropriate document tools for format/render verification; a text scan does not check layout.
- For DOCX, Markdown, or text clean drafts, run `python3 scripts/clean_draft_lint.py <file> --source <source-file>` when a source is available. Inspect each candidate in context. The script checks only selected explicit numbering and wording patterns. It does not prove analytical quality, natural Chinese, factual correctness, authorization, or content preservation. “Limited format/wording scan passed” is the strongest allowed description of a clean result; never claim the report passed professional review based on it.
- Do not pad a revision to reach 80% of source length. Investigate an unexpected reduction semantically; approval is about the actual change, not a ratio or a command-line flag.
- If the original already meets the task's requirements, say so and leave it unchanged. If no authorized clean result can be finished, disclose the exact blocker and request a decision without changing deliverable type.

For maintenance or regression evaluation of this skill, use [references/behavioral-regression.md](references/behavioral-regression.md) and its fixtures. Automated script tests are not model-behavior tests; report which were actually run.
